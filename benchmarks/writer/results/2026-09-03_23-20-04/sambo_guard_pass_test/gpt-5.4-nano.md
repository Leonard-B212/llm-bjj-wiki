# Attack (Top)

## Setup

* Works primarily from [[Closed-Guard]]
* Required initial controls:
  * Establish a strong Upper-Body-Pressure (torso pin) so the opponent’s shoulder line stays controlled
  * Create asymmetric upper-body control: Underhook on one side + Crossface on the opposite arm
  * Position your shoulder line clearly above / beyond the opponent’s shoulder line to prevent space between your hips and their hips

## Execution

* Establish the upper-body pin:
  * From [[Closed-Guard]], get a right Underhook and a left Crossface
  * Drive your head and shoulder far forward over the opponent’s head/shoulder line
  * Bring your own shoulder line above / past their shoulder line so your weight pins their torso to the mat
  * Spread your base slightly and lift your hips partway (partial “stand-up”) while keeping your upper body glued to their torso
* Transition control: manage rotation timing before removing Crossface
  * Use the Underhook to control/grip the opponent’s shoulder so they cannot generate movement for the re-guard
  * Keep constant pressure while you start to free your path for walking around
  * Only then remove (control the release of) the Crossface—avoid pulling it away “early” or the opponent regains movement
* Replace with a Shoulder Pin:
  * After the Crossface starts to release, thread/control the other arm over/around the opponent’s head area
  * Bring both arms into the shoulder zone to create a strong Shoulder Pin (torso stays pinned, hips/legs become the moving part)
* Open the guard by walking around (rotation under pressure):
  * Walk around to the left (in this example: moving around while your pinned torso resists counter-rotation)
  * The key mechanism: your underbody travels laterally while the opponent’s upper body is kept pinned, causing tension/rotation between the pinned torso and the Closed Guard
  * Do not immediately try to rip the legs apart with your hands
  * Continue circling until the Closed Guard opens by the pressure + angle change
* Optional “helping” step only if needed:
  * If a very flexible opponent keeps the guard closed even after you’ve circled far, slightly adjust your angle backward
  * Use your elbow to help break/open the Closed Guard lock
  * Keep the shoulder/upper-body control intact—don’t fully give up the Upper-Body-Pin
* Finish immediately into Knee Slice (no pause):
  * As soon as the guard opens, immediately pass to the Knee Slice
  * Keep your hand controlling the opened leg and preventing the opponent from re-threading legs to close the guard
  * Minimize the time between the guard opening and the Knee Slice so the opponent cannot reach a neutral re-guard setup
* Typical outcomes / destinations depending on reactions:
  * Primary goal is [[Side-Control]]
  * If the opponent initiates a scramble/turn (e.g., a Ghostescape-like direction), follow the movement and transition to [[North-South]] when appropriate
  * Depending on arm position, body angle, and reaction, a direct transition to [[Kesa-Gatame]] can happen

## Follow-Ups

* If the opponent tries to reorient/escape behind your line → transition to [[North-South]]
* If the angle favors it after the Shoulder Pin and Knee Slice → transition to [[Kesa-Gatame]]
* If the opponent gives you a stable top chest/side control outcome → settle into [[Side-Control]]

## Control After Pass

* Stabilize in the top control by maintaining:
  * continuous Upper-Body-Pressure during and right after the Knee Slice
  * a solid posture/base to keep the opponent’s shoulders pinned to the floor
* Prevent re-guard:
  * Control the opened leg with your hand(s) so they can’t immediately close the legs again
  * Keep your weight forward (shoulder line remains past theirs) to avoid creating hip-to-hip space

## Problems

* Shoulder line not far enough forward / wrong weight distribution:
  * If your weight sits too far back, you create space between your body and the opponent
  * Space can appear between your hips and their hips, allowing them to bring legs back inside (e.g., toward [[Butterfly-Guard]]-type re-entries)
* Taking too long between guard opening and Knee Slice:
  * The opponent can recover timing, create frames, and rebuild their guard
* Timing mistakes with Crossface:
  * Removing the Crossface without maintaining shoulder control via the Underhook can give the opponent back mobility
* Giving up the Shoulder Pin too early:
  * If you lose the torso pin, rotation/tension collapses and the opponent keeps the legs connected

## Hints

* Core principle: pin the opponent’s torso, then move your underbody around them
* Your Upper-Body-Pin is the “engine”—the pass should not rely on ripping the legs with force
* Timing: release Crossface only when Underhook shoulder control is already doing the job
* Movement continuity: keep the passing sequence connected
  * Closed Guard → Upper-Body-Pin → open under pressure by walking around → immediate Knee Slice → dominant top position
* Avoid “neutral space”: no long pauses, no loose hip placement during the transition
* Pressure matters more than brute strength; use posture and angle to make the guard open structurally
